## Overview

Color plays an essential role in shaping the visual language of UNI, establishing hierarchy, enhancing usability, and reinforcing brand identity. The UNI Design System’s color framework is built around four key categories: **Semantics**, **Neutrals**, **Accents**, and **Brand**. Each category has been meticulously crafted to ensure consistency, accessibility, and a cohesive user experience across all products.

## Color Categories

### Semantic Colors

Semantic colors are used to communicate meaning or status to the user. They ensure that important information, like success, warnings, or errors, is conveyed clearly and consistently.

  
![semantics](/images/c511e13e-0bee-4dbc-9d6e-ab6ac908b028.png)

### Neutral Colors

Neutral colors provide a balanced foundation for the design. They are typically used for backgrounds, text, dividers, and components where content needs to stand out without overpowering the primary or semantic colors.

  
![neutral](/images/ee2a997c-d360-40d5-a455-34740e6d3eec.png)

### Accent Colors

Accent colors are used sparingly to draw attention to specific elements or interactions. These colors are often employed to complement brand and neutral colors, adding emphasis without detracting from the overall visual hierarchy.

  
![accents](/images/78b0495b-bd71-4105-811e-a77d6c91a9e5.png)

### Brand Colors

Brand colors represent UNI’s visual identity and are the most recognizable part of our design language. They are used prominently in key actions, backgrounds, and elements that require strong brand association.

## Accessibility & Contrast Ratios

Accessibility is a top priority for UNI. All text, icons, and interactive elements must meet WCAG 2.1 standards for color contrast to ensure readability for users with visual impairments.

### Minimum Contrast Ratios

To meet accessibility guidelines, color contrast must adhere to the following minimum ratios:

- **Body Text:** 4.5:1 contrast against the background.

- **Large Text (18px bold or 24px regular):** 3:1 contrast against the background.

- **Icons and UI elements:** 3:1 contrast against the background.

## Color States

Interactive components often require multiple visual states (e.g., hover, pressed, disabled) to indicate their behavior to the user. Each of these states has specific color requirements:

- **Hover State:** Slightly darker or lighter shade of the primary or secondary color.

- **Active State:** A border or saturated version of the primary color to indicate interaction.

- **Disabled State:** Muted neutral tones with reduced opacity to indicate inaction.

## Best Practices

To maintain visual harmony and brand consistency, follow these best practices:

- **Limit Color Usage:** Use no more than 3-4 colors in any interface to avoid overwhelming the user.

- **Test for Accessibility:** Ensure all color combinations meet contrast ratio guidelines.

- **Maintain Consistency:** Stick to the defined color palette and avoid introducing new, unapproved colors.

- **Color Psychology:** Be mindful of the emotional and psychological impact of colors on users (e.g., red for danger, green for success).
